This example includes a blog and a changelog. They share a theme and navigation, but each gives you a different way to tell readers what is happening.

## Use the blog for context

A blog post is a place to explain a decision, walk through a workflow or share what your team has learned. Give the reader enough background to understand why the topic matters.

For a feature launch, that might mean describing the problem, showing an example and explaining who the feature is for.

## Use the changelog for the details

A release note should make it easy to see what changed. A short list of additions, improvements and fixes is often enough.

Put release notes in `changelog/`. Like blog posts, each entry has a title and date. You can also include a version and tags to identify the release.

## Keep only the sections you need

You can publish both sections or use just the blog. Set their paths under your site's content settings in the dashboard.

The content folders stay named `blog/` and `changelog/`, even if you change their public paths. This keeps your files organized without tying them to a particular domain or URL structure.
