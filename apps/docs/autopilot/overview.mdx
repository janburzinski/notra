Autopilot is the part of Notra that acts on your numbers. Once a day it looks at the questions AI engines and Google answer without you, what your Agent Readiness scan flagged and which pull requests you merged. When it finds something worth changing, it writes the change and opens a draft pull request in your website repository. A brief in Slack tells you what's waiting.

## How it helps

GEO tracking shows you which questions AI engines answer without mentioning you. Getting into those answers takes a post for each question a competitor owns, updates to pages engines stop citing, and an `llms.txt` and structured data that agents can read. Most teams know what to do and rarely get to it. Autopilot does this work every day, starting from your own data.

Posts start from demand. Autopilot writes for a prompt engines answer without you, or a search where you show up but don't win, and each post targets one question. The post's page in Notra then shows whether your share of answers moved. Shipped features become posts too, once people ask about the topic.

It also looks after what you already have. When engines cite a page half as often as last month, Autopilot edits that page against what they cite now. When your readiness scan finds a missing `llms.txt` or AI crawler rules, or content that only renders with JavaScript, it fixes that in your repository.

The longer it runs, the closer its drafts get to what you would write. Close a draft with "we don't write about pricing" and it stops suggesting pricing posts. Change the headings in two drafts the same way and the next draft follows. See [How Autopilot learns](/autopilot/learning).

## How a day goes

<Steps>
  <Step title="Autopilot runs at your hour">
    At the hour you pick (09:00 by default, in your timezone), Autopilot reads your data and runs every automation that is on. If one fails, the others still run.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It opens draft pull requests">
    Work with enough evidence becomes a draft pull request on a branch that starts with `notra/autopilot/`. A post it isn't sure about is saved as an idea for you to write or drop.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You get the daily brief">
    The brief in Slack starts with the item to look at first, then lists what's waiting on you, what merged since the last brief and anything worth knowing. React 👍 to merge a pull request or 👎 to close it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You review it, change it or let it publish">
    Read the post on its Autopilot page, ask for changes in a sentence, merge it or close it with a reason. With approvals on Auto, Autopilot publishes later pull requests itself at 09:00 on the first weekday at least 24 hours away, unless someone steps in.
  </Step>
</Steps>

[How a run works](/autopilot/how-it-works) covers the full order and how Autopilot decides what to write.

## What it works on

Autopilot has eight built-in [automations](/autopilot/automations/overview), and a first setup turns all of them on. They fix agent readiness and pages AI crawlers can't read, write posts for content gaps, comparison pages and releases, and keep existing pages current with refreshes, internal links and landing page copy. For anything else you want watched, like a competitor passing you in AI answers, describe it in the agent dock and Autopilot builds a [workflow](/autopilot/workflows).

## What stays in your hands

Autopilot never commits to your default branch. A change is merged when you merge it, when you react 👍 in Slack, or when its approval group is set to Auto. Even on Auto, the first pull request of each group (new posts, page updates, readiness fixes) waits for a person, and so does anything with unsourced claims or files outside your content folder.

Facts in posts come from your pages, your brand identity, the evidence Autopilot collected and the pages it read while writing. Claims without a source are flagged in the pull request for you to check. Autopilot doesn't change `package.json`, lockfiles, environment files or CI, and it doesn't add dependencies. It keeps at most three of its own pull requests open and closes drafts nobody touched after 14 days. See [Review and publish](/autopilot/review-and-publish).

## Where to find it

Switch the sidebar to **Studio** and open **Autopilot**. The **Automations** tab shows the automations that are on and templates you haven't added, **Pull requests** lists what Autopilot opened, **Analytics** shows how much of it you merged, and **Settings** holds where it publishes, the brief, approvals and what to ignore. The agent dock at the bottom answers questions about runs and automations.

<Note>
  Only workspace owners and admins can set up, run and change Autopilot. Members can see its pull requests, analytics and runs. If **Autopilot** isn't in your sidebar, it isn't available for your workspace yet. Contact us to get access.
</Note>

## What it costs

You pay when Autopilot opens a pull request, or changes one because you asked. Each costs what its model calls cost, taken from your plan's pull request credits first and then from AI credits. Planning, rescans, measuring results and the brief are free, so a day with nothing to change costs nothing. See [Limits and credits](/autopilot/limits-and-credits).
